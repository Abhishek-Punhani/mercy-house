## Dr. Mira Sen (mira)

Mira has spent much of her working life being the person summoned after a decision has already become irreversible. At Mercy House she learned the language of registers, where a crowded night could be reduced to a neat line, and later learned how long an omitted sentence could continue to trouble its witness. Her return to the town is deliberate. She has carried records back with her, not certainty that the people named in them will welcome her.

## Savitri Das (savitri)

For years Savitri could enter a crowded ward and make everyone lower their voices. She remembers each winter by what had to be rationed and each household by who came asking for help. At home she has preserved the smaller habits of authority: a straightened tablecloth, a cup placed beyond a sleeve, a conversation redirected before Leena hears its most difficult part. The old ward history and Mira's threatened publication belong to her own private recollections. She fears what their disclosure will do to the life she has maintained around her daughter.

**Visible appearance:** A woman in a carefully mended dark dress keeps her hands folded around a cup that has gone cold. Her speech is measured; occasionally she corrects a small detail before answering the larger question.

## Professor Vedant Rao (vedant)

Vedant has arranged his home so he can work without asking someone else to move a chair. The accessible passages and speaking tubes at Mercy House began as practical solutions, and he remains proud of understanding what a building permits people to hear. His research, his old institutional work and his promises to Savitri do not sit comfortably together. He responds to that discomfort by becoming exact about words, as though an exact sentence could put the rest of an evening back in order.

**Visible appearance:** A scholar sits beside a workbench in a wheelchair, one hand resting on a drawing weight. He pauses to choose precise words and asks that people speak to him directly.

## Dev Malik (dev)

Dev knows the cemetery by the work it asks of his hands. He can tell which ground will flood and which family will arrive without money for a marker. His unauthorized errands for the bereaved are a source of shame as well as stubborn justification. At home Bina has made winter beds from poor soil and salvaged slate. He wants to reach that house at the end of an ordinary evening, and wants fewer questions about how he earns the kindness of grieving people.

**Visible appearance:** A groundsman holds his cap by the brim. Soil has dried in the creases of his hands; he answers practical questions more readily than questions about his reputation.

Age 48; gravedigger and caretaker. Home: garden.

## Sera Dutt (sera)

Sera measures an evening in kettles, pans and the time left before the last guest asks for food. Wages go to her mother's care before they become her own. Once, telling an employer something unwelcome cost her a position; she remembers the weeks that followed more clearly than the dispute itself. She is capable of careful observation, but being made responsible for an interpretation frightens her. She needs room to say that she noticed one thing without agreeing that it proves another.

**Visible appearance:** The cook wipes hands that are already dry on the edge of her apron. Her eyes return to the nearest door when voices rise. She starts a sentence twice before choosing its first word.

Age 29; cook. Home: inn.

**Family:** mother: Elsie; younger brother: Pawan.

Her mother remains a familiar point of order in a town where much else has changed.
