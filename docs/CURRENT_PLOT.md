# The Mercy House case — author review

Private authoring context. The detective receives its remembered life and local scene in [DETECTIVE_OPENING.md](DETECTIVE_OPENING.md), rather than this reconstruction. November 1900 has closed the mountain road above Bhairav’s Hollow. Inspector Arun Mehra returns on leave after Dr. Mira Sen asks to discuss a family record. Twelve winters earlier, his sister Tara died at Mercy House. The official letter called it fever. Arun remembers unanswered requests, Raghav Sethi’s signature on institutional replies, and Savitri Das setting tea before him after the funeral. His instructions never tell him whom to distrust.

Leena Das was once his partner. She is now privately involved with Mira. Her mother, Savitri, was the matron who refused Tara release and later removed that refusal from the copied statement. Vedant Rao, an acoustics scholar who uses a wheelchair, maintained institutional records and removed Nera from a replacement death register. His disability is an ordinary biographical and accessibility fact; the evidence concerns his actions. Raghav’s responsibility in Tara’s confinement is distinct from the current murder.

Mira has brought the original statement back. She means to leave on the dawn coach and release it. Savitri fears exposure and the loss of her daughter’s regard. Vedant agrees to keep their meeting private despite knowing Savitri has threatened violence. The engine executes his obstruction, Savitri’s entrance and assault, and the traces left by the fatal injury. It then executes Savitri writing a threat in Dev Malik’s name. The planted document is an object to investigate, not a truthful narrator’s statement.

The older burial subplot is authored starting history. Savitri commissioned Nalin to move two grave crates into Dev and Bina’s winter beds. Nalin’s private appeal to his brother Kabir and an independently retained invoice document the journey. A search can reveal disturbed ground; recovery requires the spade and excavation. The location of the remains does not establish that Dev killed anyone.

Kabir is Arun’s colleague of six years, once rescued by him from a flooded cellar. He is also Nalin’s older brother. During an unsupervised delegated search he can conceal an unrecorded letter exposing Nalin. His character agent chooses from legal actions using its private situation; it cannot delete an already secured discovery by saying it disappeared. The investigation begins at the body. The detective may inspect the room, identify those present, ask its own questions, seek access, travel, collect material and compare acquired records. A household worker knows household routines; a bystander knows an observation or rumour. Neither receives the causal journal. Reassurance and relevant material can make a frightened person willing to answer. A question cannot grant access to another character’s memories or the verifier.

Forest sounds, the closed ward and Ishan’s uncertain impressions supply unease. A ghost controlling the killers, further suspicious deaths and fully autonomous offstage lifetimes are **not implemented**. They are not facts the detective must guess. A future supernatural mechanism needs explicit transition rules and discoverable support before becoming a scored conclusion.

The full inquest has seven material targets: fatal assault, knowing assistance, fabrication of the threat, commissioning grave relocation, carrying it out, alteration of Tara’s statement and alteration of the death register. Each requires acquired proof. Private relationships and unresolved explanations may be discussed in narrative; narrative is not automatically fact-checked. Concealment has a separate task because it depends on search history.
