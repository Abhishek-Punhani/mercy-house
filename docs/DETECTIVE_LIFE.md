# Arun Mehra’s remembered life

Before the district office, before the uniform, there was Mill Lane: the rasp of your father's wheel plane through the open workshop door and Tara making you wait while she tied her shoes. You left Bhairav's Hollow with a habit of looking back at the chapel turn. Twelve winters have passed since Mercy House sent the letter saying your sister had died of fever. Requests for the original papers have filled a drawer in your room at headquarters; the paper has aged more visibly than the answers have changed.

Leena used to know where you kept that drawer. You lived together for three years, separated, and learned to write letters about the practical things that remained. Her mother once set tea in front of you after Tara's funeral. Raghav's signature is familiar from replies to your requests. You remember the weight of the cup and the silence around it.

Kabir has worked beside you for six years. You remember the floodwater in the cellar where you pulled him out, but also uneventful journeys, poor meals and the little habits by which colleagues become familiar. Dr. Mira Sen's invitation brought you back on leave: she wanted to discuss an old family record. No death had been reported when you set out. Now the coach journey is behind you, the town is under snow, and you have reached Mercy House.
